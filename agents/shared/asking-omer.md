# Asking Omer and consulting other models

Rules for a hard gate put to Omer, a decision the session settles on its own, or a second opinion. `core.md` (Working) defines a hard gate and holds the rule that agents own every other decision.

- Consult another model only when Omer, the repository, the skill you follow, or `handoffs.md` asks for it; start a Codex consult with `codex-ask advise <checkout> <brief-file> <out-dir>`, whose usage header gives the model, effort, and wait flags, send one brief per batch of related choices, and check the claims its answer rests on against the source before you use it. When a problem stays unsolved, such as a second failed fix with the cause still unknown, or a design trade-off that no source settles, and `command -v oracle` succeeds on this host, name an Oracle consult in one line of the message that carries your next tool call ("An Oracle consult, GPT-6 Pro in ChatGPT, could settle this; say yes to run it"), keep working on the strongest path you have, and run the oracle skill only after Omer says yes, because each consult spends his ChatGPT Pro allowance.
- Before you settle a choice that weighs a technical, legal, or compliance trade-off, such as fewer bytes downloaded against a change in what users see, research it in primary sources and on the web, and put what you found and the claims you checked in the record of the decision. When the choice is how a site or app shows something to its users, such as a legal notice, a banner, or a consent step, also open comparable live sites in the same market with a headless browser and put what they show in that record, because Omer judges such a choice against what peers ship.
- A question that asks Omer to spend money or accept a recurring charge (a purchase, a plan, a subscription) says what the item is in one clause, why the work needs it, and each cheaper way with the reason it is not the pick, and ends with numbered options (`voice.md`). Each number names its source (`core.md`, Verification). Bad: "Three KMS keys at 3 USD a month, or one shared key at 1 USD?" Good: "Cognito encrypts the sign-in code with a key we own, a KMS key at 1 USD a month each (aws.amazon.com/kms/pricing). Cheaper ways are one shared key for 1 USD a month, Cognito's built-in sender with its cap of 50 mails a day per AWS account (docs.aws.amazon.com/cognito/latest/developerguide/quotas.html), and Amazon SES, which is usage-priced and needs AWS's production-access approval. 1. Three keys, 3 USD a month (recommended): each environment has its own key policy. 2. One shared key, 1 USD a month: one key policy serves all three environments. 3. Amazon SES: our spec has decided against it."
- Put a hard gate (`core.md`, Working) to Omer only once the result it approves is ready, so the yes starts only the approved action. When the approval gates a merge, a deploy, a publication, or a production write, the change has finished the Testing order up to landing before you ask, and a plan puts the question after those steps; a write that lands without a PR has its exact command ready, with its dry-run output when the tool has a dry-run mode. Preserve explicit approval requirements. Do not treat elapsed time or silence as approval. Bad: a plan step "the drop migration runs as its own PR after Omer's yes". Good: "the drop migration deletes production data, so its PR is gated and reviewed before the question, and Omer's yes merges it."
- When a plan's decision, or a choice you put to Omer, rests on runtime behaviour or a live setting that a check on the target can show in a few minutes, run that check first with every access that the "Before you name a step for Omer" bullet in `core.md` (Working) lists, and cite its output. Source shows one layer; a run shows what all the layers do together. When the work later disproves a fact under a choice Omer made, or approved by name such as an option he picked, such as finding that a chosen feature must be turned off or that a package builds only with a workaround, choose again with the new fact: take the option that best serves the intent he stated, put both facts and the new choice in the first lines of the report, and continue, because Omer reviews the switch afterwards. When the new option crosses a hard gate, such as an action his chat forbids, it waits on Needs you like any hard gate. When it disproves a fact under a decision you made, including one you wrote into a plan he approved as a whole, decide again, record the new fact and the new decision in the plan or report with its trade-off and how to reverse it, and continue.
