## Legal basis (Art. 5) and principles (Art. 4)

Art. 4 requires processing to be lawful, accurate, for specific and legitimate purposes, limited to what the purpose needs, and kept only as long as the purpose or a law requires. In code: collect only the fields a purpose uses, and give each a retention period.

Art. 5(2) lists the bases other than explicit consent: a law requires it; vital interest; (c) performing a contract with the person; (ç) a legal obligation of the controller; the person made the data public; (e) establishing or defending a right; (f) the controller's legitimate interest, without harming the person's rights. Use consent only when none of these fits. The Board's principle decision 2026/347 says a controller that relies on another basis gives the privacy notice only and asks for no consent. Typical consent cases are marketing messages, non-essential cookies, and an occasional transfer abroad.

## Special category data (Art. 6)

Art. 6(1): race, ethnic origin, political opinion, philosophical belief, religion or other belief, appearance and dress, membership of associations, foundations, or unions, health, sexual life, criminal convictions and security measures, biometric and genetic data.

Law No. 7499 (in force 01-06-2024) replaced Art. 6(3). Processing is forbidden except for (a) explicit consent; (b) a law requires it; (c) vital interest of someone who cannot consent; (ç) data the person made public, within that intent; (d) establishing, using, or protecting a right; (e) health care purposes by people under a duty of confidentiality; (f) legal obligations in employment, occupational safety, and social security; (g) non-profit foundations and associations, for their members. One list now covers health and sexual life too.

Art. 6(4) requires the extra measures of Board decision 2018/10: encryption, access logs, and staff training among them.

## Privacy notice and explicit consent (Art. 10)

Art. 10 requires telling the person, at collection: the controller's (and representative's) identity, the purpose, who receives the data and why, the collection method and legal basis, and the Art. 11 rights. The Communiqué on the duty to inform (RG 10-03-2018) asks for plain language and specific purposes.

Board decision 2018/90 and principle decision 2026/347: the privacy notice and the consent request are separate texts with separate actions. One checkbox for both is unlawful. Giving the notice needs no approval; the form may record that it was shown or read, never consent to it.

Explicit consent is specific, informed, and freely given (Art. 3). The cookie guide asks for an active, affirmative act, and the commercial message regulation forbids a pre-selected consent (Art. 7(8)), so silence, a pre-ticked box, or using the site is not consent.

## Transfer abroad (Art. 9)

Art. 9 as replaced by Law No. 7499 allows a transfer abroad on one of these routes, in order:

1. An Art. 5 or 6 basis plus an adequacy decision. The Board has made none for any country: its transfer page says "Bu konuda Kurul tarafından henüz bir belirleme yapılmamıştır."
2. An Art. 5 or 6 basis plus an appropriate safeguard. For a company, that is a standard contract, or binding corporate rules approved by the Board.
3. An occasional transfer under Art. 9(6), such as explicit consent after being told the risks. The 2024 transfer regulation (Art. 16) limits this to transfers that are irregular, one-off, and outside the normal course of business, so a product feature that calls a service abroad cannot use it.

The standard contract (2024 transfer regulation, Art. 14):

- The Board publishes four texts. A vendor that processes data for the product, such as an AI or email API, takes Standard Contract 2 (controller to processor).
- The text is used without any change; the Turkish text governs.
- It is filed with the Authority within 5 business days of the last signature, physically or by KEP. The exporter files unless the contract names the other party. A foreign-language document needs a notarised translation.
- Not filing is fined under Art. 18(1)(d): 90.308 to 1.806.177 TL in 2026 (KVKK fine table, revalued yearly).

A vendor's DPA built on the EU standard contractual clauses is not a KVKK standard contract.

## Rights and requests (Art. 11, 13)

Art. 11(1) rights: learn whether data is processed and get information about it; learn the purpose; know the recipients in Türkiye and abroad; correction; deletion or destruction under Art. 7; have corrections and deletions passed to recipients; object to a result against the person that comes only from automated analysis (g); compensation.

Art. 13(2): the controller answers a request as soon as possible and within 30 days, free of charge unless the action has a separate cost under the Board's tariff.

## Security and breaches (Art. 12)

Art. 12(1) requires technical and administrative measures against unlawful processing and access. The controller is jointly liable with its processors.

Board decision 2019/10 sets the breach notice: to the Board without delay and within 72 hours of learning of the breach, and to the affected people within the shortest reasonable time. A late notice gives the reasons for the delay. Processors tell the controller without delay.

## Retention and deletion

Regulation on Deletion, Destruction or Anonymization of Personal Data:

- Controllers that must register in VERBIS write a retention and destruction policy (Art. 5).
- Periodic destruction runs at an interval set in that policy, at most 6 months (Art. 11(2)). Without a policy, data is destroyed within 3 months after the duty arises (Art. 11(3)).
- A person's deletion request is completed within 30 days (Art. 12).
- Destruction records are kept for at least 3 years (Art. 7(3)).

## VERBIS and foreign companies

VERBIS is the register of data controllers. Board decision 2025/1572 exempts controllers with fewer than 50 employees and an annual balance sheet under 100 million TL whose main activity is not processing special category data, and those whose main activity is special category data with fewer than 10 employees and a balance sheet under 10 million TL. Decision 2025/2393: a controller without balance-sheet books is judged by employee count alone.

A controller not resident in Türkiye registers in VERBIS before processing, through a representative resident in Türkiye, whatever its size (Regulation on the Registry of Data Controllers, Art. 5(1)(b) and Art. 11).
